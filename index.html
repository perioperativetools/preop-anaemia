Pre-operative Hb / Anaemia – Nurse Action & Escalation Tool

Static GitHub Pages demonstrator. Adult elective/scheduled pre-operative pathway only.

Not for paediatric, obstetric, cardiac/cardiothoracic, emergency surgery or active haemorrhage. Purpose: help the pre-op nurse recognise Hb significance, complete the anaemia bundle, initiate agreed pathway actions, and know when to escalate. The nurse does not prescribe transfusion or make the final proceed/postpone decision.
Nurse input
1. Select operation risk + special considerations
Operation bleeding / PBM risk
LOW: minor/low-loss procedures. INTERMEDIATE: moderate procedures. MAJOR/HIGH: major surgery / substantial blood-loss pathway. Examples: Low—cataract, superficial/minor plastic. Intermediate—breast, hernia, lap chole, therapeutic endoscopy, endoscopic urology, non-obstetric gynae, minor/intermediate ENT/maxfax. Major/High—joint replacement, major colorectal/ upper GI/HPB/urology/gynae/vascular/head & neck/reconstruction.
Sex
Used for the formal diagnostic Hb label. Major/high surgery still uses Hb <130 g/L as an optimisation trigger.
Cancer / chemotherapy
Flag time-sensitive cancer care; optimisation should run in parallel and avoid unnecessary oncological delay.
CKD
May contribute to functional iron restriction. Significant anaemia / ESA questions need specialist input.
Bariatric / malabsorption
Oral iron may be poorly absorbed; B12/folate or other deficiencies may coexist.
Refuses donor blood products
Needs early consultant anaesthesia + transfusion/haematology planning and explicit documentation of acceptable alternatives.
Known red-cell antibodies / difficult blood
Notify the transfusion laboratory early; compatible units may require additional lead time.
Known sickle cell disease / significant haemoglobinopathy
Use the dedicated haematology/haemoglobinopathy pathway; generic Hb bands are not sufficient for transfusion planning.
Days to planned surgery
Current CPOC: ≥4 weeks usually allows oral iron; <4 weeks prompts consideration of IV iron or postponement. Follow current pre-op Hb-SOP.
Current oral-iron status
Helps decide whether the nurse should initiate/continue oral treatment or refer for IV-iron assessment.
2. Start with Hb + trend
Current Hb (g/L)
Hb bands: ≥130; 120–129; 100–119; 80–99; <80.
Previous Hb (ideally within ~6 months)
If no previous result is visible, check GP / primary-care records where accessible.
Hb trend
A falling trend can be more important than a single isolated Hb value.
3. Ensure / enter full anaemia bundle
MCV
Normal MCV does not exclude iron deficiency.
MCH
Low MCH can support iron-restricted erythropoiesis; do not use alone.
WBC
Abnormal counts with anaemia may indicate a broader haematological problem.
Platelets
Multiple cytopenias / abnormal counts should not be managed as simple IDA.
Ferritin (µg/L)
<30 strongly supports absolute iron deficiency; ferritin may rise with inflammation.
TSAT (%)
<20% suggests restricted available iron; interpret with ferritin, CRP, eGFR and context.
CRP (mg/L)
>5 mg/L supports an inflammatory context when interpreting iron studies.
eGFR
<60 can support CKD / functional iron-restriction context.
Vitamin B12
Use local laboratory reference range.
Folate
Use local laboratory reference range.
Reticulocyte Hb / CHr (if available)
Low CHr/Ret-He supports iron-restricted erythropoiesis; optional where available.
Blood film
If abnormal, or if cytopenias/unclear anaemia are present, seek medical/haematology review.
Haemolysis suspected / markers abnormal
If yes, do not route as simple IDA; seek medical/haematology review.
